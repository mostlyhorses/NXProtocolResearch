The process of defining a new lens starts with deciding on the features that will be supported. The features determine the Operations, Properties, Specific Data, Events, and Status that the lens needs to support and provide.

The camera expects the lenses to behave in a particular way, so for each feature they need to do specific things, E.g. responding to requests with particular status codes, actuating the focus mechanism only when enabled by the body, emitting events and status.

The exact meaning of some parts of messages is not known, but by comparing the data from many lenses it should be possible to use responses from existing lenses with similar functionality.

There are also some operations that the lenses claim support, but which have not been observed, e.g. 1005/1009/100E/... . Since they've not been observed hopefully they can be left out, but it is probably sufficient to claim support and then not do anything.

The following Chapter 10 subsections describe in more detail the behavior of each operation, event, and status; but refer back to Chapters 4-8 for the data formats. The features may define properties that are referred to in other sections, e.g. \[C1\] is 'standard SPI clock rate'; and variables that hold dynamic information about the lens, e.g. \[ZZ1\] the current zoom point.

- 'Y' means the thing needs to be implemented
- 'N' means it doesn't appear that the thing needs to be implemented
- 'Y/n' means most provide it but some don't, but don't know why.
- 'y/N' means probably can get away without it
- '+r' in the Property section indicates that that module responds to the 0x1007 and 0x1006 ops, not just 0x1006 like the others.
- The 'Unknown' column means we don't know what feature the thing relates to, but it may still be necessary to implement it
